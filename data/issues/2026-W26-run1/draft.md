## 1. AI in Finance

**KPMG pulled its agentic AI report after hallucinated citations were exposed**

> **A Big Four firm that audits AI governance for clients failed its own standard in public, giving CFO teams a concrete benchmark for what "adequate human oversight" must mean in practice.** GPTZero found that only 5 of 45 citations in KPMG's October 2025 agentic AI report pointed to their stated sources; the rest ranged from mangled to partially fabricated, a pattern GPTZero called "vibe citing." KPMG pulled the report on June 13 and acknowledged its responsible-AI guidelines had not been followed. Apply the same citation-verification step to any AI-generated analysis, board paper, or regulatory filing your team produces or receives.
>
> Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/13/kpmg-pulls-report-on-ai-usage-due-to-apparent-hallucinations/

---

**KPMG deploys Microsoft Agent 365 to govern AI agents across 276,000 staff**

> **KPMG's global rollout of agent governance infrastructure is the clearest enterprise signal yet that AI agent management and audit controls are now a board-level requirement.** Microsoft Agent 365, generally available since May 2026, gives organisations a unified control plane to manage, monitor, and audit agents built on any stack including Microsoft, Anthropic, or proprietary models. KPMG's deployment at this scale normalises the expectation that every agent in a finance workflow must be traceable and auditable.
>
> Source: https://news.microsoft.com/source/2026/06/09/kpmg-and-microsoft-scale-trusted-enterprise-ai-agents-globally-through-deployment-of-agent-365-and-copilot/

**Gartner tells CFOs to build structured finance AI roadmaps**

> **84% of finance organisations have adopted or plan to adopt AI, yet only 7% report high or very high impact — the execution gap is now the central CFO-office risk.** Gartner's new finance AI maturity model defines five phases (avoid, experiment, stabilise, expand, transform) across four workstreams: culture, strategy, skills, and software. The model gives the CFO's team a vendor-neutral audit framework to prioritise next investments rather than react to individual tool announcements.
>
> Source: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-08-gartner-says-cfos-need-structured-finance-ai-roadmaps

**Bottomline launches CFO Suite, an agentic AI platform for treasury and cash flow**

> **Port operators with multi-bank, multi-currency cash complexity can now layer agentic treasury AI over existing systems without replacing the underlying ERP or treasury management system.** Bottomline's CFO Suite, launched June 11, connects forecasting, accounts payable (AP), accounts receivable (AR), outbound payments, and cash application inside a single modular platform powered by BEA, a finance-first agentic engine. Auditable, human-in-the-loop outputs are built in, reducing both deployment risk and time to value compared to an ERP-native rollout.
>
> Source: https://www.bottomline.com/newsroom/press-releases/bottomline-unveils-cfo-suite-bring-together-governed-ai-and-end-end-cash-flow-management

**Forrester Wave names leaders in AP invoice automation**

> **The agentic tier in AP automation now creates a material capability gap over rules-based incumbents, making this an active re-evaluation trigger for any CFO whose current contract is approaching renewal.** Forrester's Q2 2026 Wave for Accounts Payable Invoice Automation evaluated 15 vendors across 26 criteria; leading platforms autonomously triage invoices, recommend resolution paths, surface early-payment discount opportunities, and prompt supplier engagement before bottlenecks escalate. CFOs evaluating or re-tendering AP automation have a current independent benchmark to anchor the process.
>
> Source: https://www.forrester.com/blogs/announcing-the-forrester-wave-accounts-payable-invoice-automation-software-q2-2026/

**Uber exhausted its 2026 AI budget in four months, then imposed a $1,500 per-employee monthly cap**

> **This is the first major enterprise case study on AI cost governance failure at scale, and it carries a direct warning for any organisation scaling agentic tools without per-user spend controls.** Uber burned its entire annual AI coding-tools budget by April 2026 after incentivising usage through internal leaderboards; individual engineers reached $500–$2,000 per month in token consumption on tools including Claude Code and Cursor. The company has now capped spend at $1,500 per employee per month and Uber's COO is publicly questioning the return on investment.
>
> Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/02/uber-caps-employee-ai-spending-after-blowing-through-budget-in-four-months/

## 2. AI Agents & Applications

**Microsoft Copilot Cowork launches with metered per-task pricing**

> **Microsoft's shift to consumption billing for agentic AI means organisations without spend guardrails configured are being charged from day one of general availability.** Copilot Cowork, released June 16, prices at $0.01 per Copilot Credit, where each credit represents one completed agentic task; billing is metered at user, department, and organisation level with no grace period. The Uber overspend and this pricing change in the same fortnight make a CFO-owned AI spend control policy an immediate operational priority.
>
> Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2026/06/16/copilot-cowork-is-now-generally-available/

---

**McKinsey: agentic AI can run banking tasks 100 times faster than human-only processes**

> **The 100x speed figure gives CFOs a concrete input for modelling staffing and productivity scenarios across finance workflows that mirror banking operations.** McKinsey's June 2026 explainer argues that orchestration frameworks have simplified enough in 2026 that banks are moving from task automation to agents owning full front-to-back workflows in credit, onboarding, compliance, and customer engagement, with declining human oversight over time. Finance teams running equivalent workflows in treasury, AP/AR, and reporting should model the same trajectory.
>
> Source: https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/mckinsey-explainers/banking-and-ai-when-the-tech-starts-doing-the-work-not-just-assisting-it

## 3. Physical AI

**APSEZ commits $100M to deploy AI across 15 terminals with disclosed productivity benchmarks**

> **A peer port operator has published specific AI capex, productivity targets, and capacity-unlock figures that CFOs and boards can use directly as reference points for their own automation business cases.** Adani Ports and Special Economic Zone (APSEZ) expanded its partnership with Kaleris on June 16 to roll out AI-augmented terminal operations across 15 container terminals in 9 ports, targeting a 20% improvement in rubber tyred gantry (RTG) crane productivity, 14% improvement in terminal truck productivity, and 91 million metric tonnes (MMT) of additional throughput capacity by 2030. The $100M two-phase commitment also sets a comparable capex anchor for board-level investment approvals.
>
> Source: https://www.adaniports.com/newsroom/media-releases/apsez-expands-partnership-with-us-based-kaleris-to-accelerate-ai-led-transformation

## 4. Foundation Models

**US government export control order suspended Anthropic model access for foreign nationals overnight**

> **A government directive can remove access to a frontier AI model within hours, with no advance notice to enterprise customers building production workflows on it.** On June 13 the White House issued an export control directive requiring Anthropic to block foreign national access to its Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models, citing jailbreak claims Anthropic publicly disputes; the company also faces a separate supply-chain blacklist tied to its refusal to permit autonomous-weapons use. Any finance-agent workflow dependent on a single model provider needs a tested multi-model fallback architecture and a documented continuity plan before this risk materialises.
>
> Source: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c932g3v3e13o
